# Discovery Instruments — Family History Album

Companion to `prd/PRD.md` §17. Each instrument is numbered to match the discovery plan (D1–D10). They are templates: use them as-is or adapt, but keep the *record* — filled-in sheets are the raw material for the PRD's v1 scope.

**Cadence note:** D1–D3 should happen within the first two weeks. The curator is the last living narrator (PRD §2); the harvest is time-bound, everything else is not.

---

## 1. Box Sampling & Inventory (D1)

**Method.** Pick three boxes that represent the collection's variety: one letters-heavy (e.g., 1962–64), one mixed, one photos/heirlooms. Inventory each fully. Don't read — count, date, note structure.

**Per-box inventory table** (one per box):

| Box ref | Type | Count | Date span | Gaps | Notable structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Box 3` | letters | 96 | Feb 1963 – Dec 1964 | none | all with envelopes; some airmail |
| `Box 3` | photos | 41 | 1960s–70s | | 12 have back-writing |
| … | | | | | |

**Structural observations** (fill once per box):

- % letters that kept their envelope / postmark: ____
- % photos with writing on the back: ____
- Average pages per letter: ____ (sample 10)
- Handwriting legibility spot-check (sample 10 letters, rate 1 = unreadable → 5 = clear): ____
- Non-paper items found (heirlooms, reels, cassettes, medals, textiles): ____
- Condition red flags (mould, water damage, fading): ____

**Projection.** Multiply the three boxes' counts to estimate collection totals. Flag which boxes were counted vs. estimated.

## 2. Effort Math (D2)

**Method.** Capture one letter exactly as the v1 ritual specifies: photograph all pages + envelope, set date, title, one line of story. Time it. Then time a second letter back-to-back (batch effect). Then compute.

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Setup (surface, lighting, tablet ready) | ____ |
| One letter, first try | ____ |
| One letter, second try (in-batch) | ____ |
| One photo (front+back) | ____ |
| One heirloom (6 shots) | ____ |

**Projection:** 830 letters × __ s + N photos × __ s + N heirlooms × __ s = **__ hours total.** Compare against your real weekly budget (e.g., 2 h/week → __ months).

**Friction log** — what felt slow, and what would make it 2× faster? (Every answer is a backlog item: e.g., "auto-advance after photo", "year wheel instead of calendar".)

## 3. Curator Story-Harvest Interview (D3) — the core instrument

**Format.** Two sessions of ~60 min, or one long one. Record audio with permission; take verbatim quotes where they land. Mark confidence on every fact: **[certain]** vs. **[remembered, verify]**. The deliverable is the raw material for bios, story collections, and captions — not a polished document.

**Warm-up (5 min).** Tell me about the loft cleanout. What did you find first? What was the moment you realized what you had?

**Block A — The cast (20 min).** The people. For each person who matters: name, relation, years alive, where they lived, and *one sentence that captures them* (their catchphrase, their habit, their worry). Include Mum and Dad as young people — the couple in the letters — and the people they wrote about. Deliverable: the cast list that seeds Cast pages and bios.

**Block B — The arc (20 min).** The story of Mum and Dad: how they met, the courtship, marriage, moves, jobs, children, the years the letters cover, and what happened after 1977. Give it chapters — "the flat search", "the wedding", "the first house". Deliverable: the story spine for Stories.

**Block C — The anchors (20 min).** Pick with your hands, not your head:
- The 3 objects you'd show a stranger first.
- The letter that makes you stop.
- The funniest artifact. The saddest.
- One heirloom, and its whole story — where it came from, whose hands it passed through.
Deliverable: the first story collections and museum highlights.

**Block D — The places (10 min).** Where they lived; where the letters came from; the places that recur ("the farm", "the bedsit"). Deliverable: the Places seed list.

**Block E — Boundaries (10 min).** Are there letters or items you don't want transcribed, or that the 5-year-old shouldn't see until he's older? Is there anything the app should *never* do? Deliverable: anti-requirements + privacy flags.

**Close.** What should the app feel like in one word? What should it never feel like?

## 4. Artifact Mining Protocol (D4)

**When:** after the first capture batch. **Sample:** 10 letters spread across 1962–77 (two per era). Read as *the person speaking* — the goal is to let them narrate again.

Per letter, extract:
- **Voice:** phrases that sound like them, that a stranger would recognize as human ("3 lines of signature flourish", "complains about the weather every week").
- **Themes:** work, money, flat-hunting, holidays, family, worry — tag each letter.
- **Names, places, events, dates** worth remembering (birthdays, anniversaries, the move).

Output: theme-tag suggestions, story seeds ("the flat search, told in 14 letters"), and on-this-day candidates (exact-date moments).

---

## 5. Secondary Narrator Interview (D5) — light touch

**When:** only if a sibling/cousin plausibly holds stories [PRD §15.8]. **Format:** 30 min, opt-in, gentle — not everyone wants to talk about dementia-era parents, and that's their call.

- What do you remember about Mum and Dad's early years — the courtship, the first flat?
- Do you have letters, photos, or objects we don't have?
- Who's who — do you know people in the photos I can't name?
- Is there a story that would be lost without you?

---

## 6. Contextual Observation Checklist (D6)

**Method.** Next time family is together, watch — don't interview. (If no gathering is near, reconstruct from memory: "what happened last time the photos came out?")

- Where does it happen? (kitchen table, sofa, in front of the TV)
- Who holds the album/tablet/phone? Who leans in?
- What do people *say*? ("oh! that's the year we…") — collect the sentence starters.
- What interrupts? (the kettle, the football, a toddler)
- What's the mood? (laughter, silence, both)

Output: the ritual the app must slot into, and the **moment-ready** events — weddings, anniversaries, Christmas, visits — where the app gets opened.

---

## 7. Future-User Legibility Review (D9)

**Method.** Role-play: you are 15, first time opening the app, you know almost nothing about this family. Walk each screen and answer honestly:

- Can I tell who these people are? (names, faces, relations — without asking anyone)
- Can I tell why this matters? (context on the page, not behind it)
- Can I tell what happened next? (the story connects, or the timeline continues)
- Is anything here only explainable by a person being present? → **that's a bug.** Fix: the narrator must be in the app (G6).

Pass = no screen fails. Run it twice: once on Home→Timeline→letter, once on a full story end-to-end.

---

## 8. Prototype Test Protocol (D8)

**Setup:** 20–30 real captures in the prototype, real tablet, observer (or record the screen). No interviewer help during tasks.

**Tasks** (give as goals, never steps):
1. "Find a letter from 1964."
2. "Find everything with Mum in it."
3. "Show me the courtship."
4. "Just have a look around for two minutes."

**Observe and note:** first tap; pauses (where and why); "oh!" moments (what caused them); confusion points; where they look for *back*; time per task.

**Debrief (after):** what surprised you? What would you want *tomorrow*? Did anything feel like homework?

---

## 9. Anti-Requirements (collected in D3-E, fed to PRD)

Nothing in the app may ever: feel like homework; require accounts; demand exact dates for approximate memories; nag; hide the person behind a metadata form; depend on a narrator who is no longer there.
